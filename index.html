Liquid chromatography

A mixture of four painkiller ingredients is pushed through a column packed with oily C18 beads. Compounds that prefer the beads over the water–acetonitrile solvent move slowly; the rest race ahead. The detector at the end draws each compound as a peak. Colours are added so you can follow the bands; the real compounds are colourless.

Things to try

  • Switch to a constant solvent: ibuprofen gets stuck. Raise the acetonitrile until it comes out, and watch paracetamol and caffeine merge.
  • Try 1.7 µm particles. Peaks sharpen, but the pressure climbs past what a normal HPLC pump can deliver.
  • Push the flow to 2 mL/min with 10 µm particles and find the dot on the van Deemter curve.

Build a mass spectrometer

Every mass spectrometer does three jobs in a row: turn molecules into ions, sort the ions by mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and count them. Pick one part for each job, choose a sample, and the instrument records a spectrum. Some combinations work far better than others.
